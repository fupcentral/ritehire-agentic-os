### 4.3 When to Mark a Lead as Dead

**Do not give up too early, but do not nurse dead leads.**

**Mark as "No Active Interest" (pause, don't delete) if:**
- No response after 4 touches (Message 1, 2, 3, Email 1) over 30+ days
- Explicit rejection ("not a fit right now")
- Company was acquired / person left role
- Responded once, then ghosted after 20+ days

**What to do with dead leads:**
- Move to "Nurture" list (email them every 6-8 weeks with new case studies/posts)
- Flag their company in CRM ("revisit Q3 2026")
- If they have strong fit: wait 2-3 months, then try again with different angle

**Do not mark as dead if:**
- Responded with any question (they're curious)
- Haven't replied yet but <30 days (give it time)
- Bounced email (may have changed job, LinkedIn search to re-qualify)

## PART 6: OPERATING PRINCIPLES (How We Win)

### 6.1 The Approach: Challenger Sale, Not Vendor Pitch

**What We Do (Don't Do This):**
```
"Hi [Name], we're RiteHire. We do Pakistan EOR. Want to talk?"
```

**What Actually Works:**
```
"Hi [Name], your team is hiring in Pakistan. 
Most companies do it informally and hit compliance risk at £8k+ payroll. 
Are you thinking about formalizing?"
```

**The Difference:**
- First version: Seller-focused (we, our product)
- Second version: Buyer-focused (their problem, their decision point)
- Second version works 3x better

**Rule:** Before any meeting, identify the buyer's pain point + their likely objection. Bring your solution as *answer to their problem*, not "here's what we sell."

### 6.2 Speed = Competitive Advantage

**Our competitors (Deel, Remote.com) are slower.**
- Deel: Great for mainstream countries, Pakistan is "exotic"
- Remote.com: Regulatory mess, 8-week onboarding

**We win on speed: 2-week setup, single point of contact, Pakistan expertise.**

**In every conversation:**
- Lead with speed (10 days vs. 8 weeks)
- Back it up with specialization (we only do Pakistan + 2-3 countries, we're obsessed)
- Remove friction (simple pricing, no surprises)

### 6.3 Volume vs. Quality

**Right now:** 
- We can't afford to be picky (£17,700 MRR is small)
- But we *can* afford to be strategic (only contact likely buyers)

**Target customer profile (rough economics):**
- Min. MRR: £1,500 (at least 3 engineers, formalized payroll)
- Max MRR: £25,000 (still manageable, no enterprise sales motion yet)
- Sweet spot: £3,500-8,000 MRR (Series A-B tech, 6-20 engineers in Pakistan)

**Volume strategy:** 
- Add 10-15 connections/week (need volume)
- Filter to green-light only (need quality)
- Expect 2-3 qualified meetings/week by April
- Convert 20-30% of meetings to proposals
- Convert 50-70% of proposals to deals
- Average deal size: £4-5k MRR

### 6.4 Measurement as a Discipline

**If we're not measuring it, we're not improving it.**

Weekly ritual (every Friday, 30 min):
1. Log metrics (connection rate, reply rate, meetings booked, MRR)
2. Identify what's working (which messages get replies? which segments?)
3. Double down on what works, kill what doesn't
4. Adjust next week

**Example:** If Tier 1 (founders/CTOs) converts at 3% and Tier 2 (HR leads) converts at 0.5%, we shift 80% of volume to Tier 1.
